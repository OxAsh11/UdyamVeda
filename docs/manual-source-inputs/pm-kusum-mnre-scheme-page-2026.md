# Manual official-source capture: PM-KUSUM MNRE scheme page

Inspected: 08 September 2026

The Ministry of New and Renewable Energy page describes PM-KUSUM through its objectives, components, beneficiaries and period. It is a three-component scheme: Component A for decentralized ground or stilt-mounted grid-connected renewable-energy plants; Component B for standalone solar agriculture pumps; and Component C for solarisation of grid-connected agriculture pumps, including feeder-level solarisation. The page states an objective of adding 34,800 MW by March 2026 with total Central Financial Support of ₹34,422 crore including service charges.

## Period and beneficiary boundaries

The page states the scheme period as “Till 31.03.2026.” Component A allows individual farmers, groups of farmers, cooperatives, panchayats, Farmer Producer Organisations and Water User Associations to set up renewable-energy plants of 500 kW to 2 MW, with developer or DISCOM implementation possible where equity cannot be arranged. Component B supports individual farmers for standalone solar agricultural pumps up to 7.5 HP in off-grid areas. Component C covers individual-pump solarisation and feeder-level solarisation under separate component conditions.

## Assistance structure

For Component B, the page states central financial assistance of 30% of benchmark or tender cost, whichever is lower, with a minimum 30% State subsidy and the remaining balance from the beneficiary or finance. It states 50% central assistance for specified special regions. Component C individual-pump solarisation follows the same 30%/50% central-assistance pattern, subject to the stated conditions. Feeder-level solarisation has separate capacity, CFA and implementation rules.

## Application and implementation

State-wise allocations and the designated implementing agencies control the route for Components B and C. The page directs users to the official PM-KUSUM portal for implementing agencies, how-to-apply information, vendors and rates. It states that sanctioned projects have a 24-month completion boundary and that releases depend on awards, completion reports, utilisation certificates and related documents.

This capture supports a closed, review-gated national record. It does not establish a post-31 March 2026 extension or a universal live application window; any state-specific continuation must be separately verified from the relevant official agency.
