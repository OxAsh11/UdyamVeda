# Trade Infrastructure for Export Scheme (TIES) revised guidelines

Official Department of Commerce guideline evidence, captured for editorial review. The document identifies the 15th Finance Commission period as FY 2021-22 to FY 2025-26.

## Objectives and scope

The objective is to improve export competitiveness by bridging gaps in export infrastructure, creating focused export infrastructure, supporting first-mile and last-mile connectivity for export-oriented projects, and addressing quality and certification measures. Assistance is extended through Central and State agencies.

The scope includes border haats, land customs stations, quality testing and certification laboratories, cold chains, trade promotion centres, dry ports, export warehousing and packaging, SEZ and cargo-terminal infrastructure, and export-logistics connectivity. General infrastructure, projects covered by sector-specific schemes, and projects without overwhelming export linkage are excluded.

## Eligible implementing agencies

Eligible implementing agencies include Central Government agencies, Export Promotion Councils, Commodity Boards, SEZ Authorities, recognised apex trade bodies, State Government agencies, qualifying government joint ventures and PPP projects. The implementing agency must own the facility and remain responsible for operation and maintenance.

The implementing agency must prepare and obtain approval for the detailed project report, obtain applicable statutory and environmental clearances, provide an operation and maintenance plan, use transparent competitive procurement, and execute the prescribed grant-utilisation undertaking. Failure to comply can require refund of the grant with applicable interest.

## Financial assistance and project conditions

Central assistance is grant-in-aid and is normally limited to the implementing agency equity or 50 percent of total project equity. For projects in North Eastern or Himalayan States, including Jammu and Kashmir and Ladakh, and certain states with poor export infrastructure and positive export potential, assistance may reach 80 percent of total equity. The normal ceiling is INR 20 crore per infrastructure project. Land cost is excluded when calculating the implementing agency contribution, and recurring or establishment expenditure is not funded.

Proposals must establish overwhelming export linkage, identify the infrastructure gap, provide measurable outcomes, describe financing and financial closure, include the detailed project report and statutory approvals, and specify operation, maintenance and monitoring arrangements. The Empowered Committee is the final authority. Disbursement follows approval and financial closure, generally in two or more instalments, with utilisation certificates, monitoring reports and matching-contribution evidence required for later releases.

The guideline describes the FY 2021-22 to FY 2025-26 period. Current implementation, claims, any extension, successor route or new proposal window must be checked separately against current Department of Commerce notices. This capture does not infer a FY 2026-27 application window.
